Chichester

Value Added v2.0 for the secondary schools of Chichester, West Sussex.

NoteAbout this page

Value Added v2.0 for Chichester’s state-funded secondary schools, 2021/22 to 2024/25, measured against England (see the method), with the analyses of the original Sussex document refitted on the v2.0 model. Built from published DfE and Ofsted data; nothing uses pupil-level records.

Summary

  • 6 schools, with value added for all pupils averaging -1.2 points against England: none significantly above expectation and 2 below.
  • Disadvantaged pupils -1.6, non-disadvantaged -1.6; girls -1.3, boys -1.1.
  • Strongest: Midhurst Rother College (+1.9). Weakest: Chichester Free School (-4.1).
  • Absence runs 0.2 points below what the intake predicts on average over 2021/22 to 2024/25; Chichester High School more than a point above.
  • No district effect can be found on the v2.0 model.
  • Suspensions: 19.8 episodes per 100 pupils (England 21.2); 7.0 pupils per 100 suspended at least once (England 7.3). Reduced timetables run at 1.6 times the national rate.
  • Trusts that stand out nationally on v2.0: The Kemnal Academies Trust (all pupils -3.1% and absence +11.3%) and United Learning Trust (all pupils +6.3% and disadvantaged pupils +8.6%).
  • Latest Ofsted: 1 report card, 2 inspections with no overall grade and 3 graded inspections.

How Chichester compares

What explains attainment in Chichester

For all pupils, Chichester is 2.4 points below England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • prior attainment (KS2) (-1.16);
  • disadvantage (+0.76);
  • absence (-0.53);
  • region (-0.47);
  • neighbourhood education (+0.41);
  • local authority (+0.37);
  • special educational needs (-0.30);
  • and the schools themselves (-1.24).

For disadvantaged pupils, Chichester is 5.0 points below England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • region (-1.22);
  • local authority (-1.04);
  • prior attainment (KS2) (-0.75);
  • English as an additional language (-0.65);
  • absence (-0.56);
  • disadvantage (-0.45);
  • admissions (selection) (-0.27);
  • and the schools themselves (+0.06).

Place: authorities and districts

The district test adds a district level to the v2.0 model, with and without each school’s own record. An effect that disappears once schools are counted belongs to particular schools; one that survives is something the district’s schools share.

None of Chichester has a district effect distinguishable from zero on the v2.0 model.

Trusts

The v2.0 model with a multi-academy trust level, allowing for each school’s own record and for how it came to be an academy, so trusts that take on struggling schools are not penalised for it. Nationally a trust moves results by about 2.4%, and absence by about 6.5%.

The disadvantaged gap, and girls and boys

No school is above expectation for its non-disadvantaged pupils while more than a point below it for its disadvantaged ones. Midhurst Rother College is above expectation for both groups. In no school do girls’ and boys’ value added differ by more than two points.

Absence

  • 8.0% absence in autumn and spring 2025/26 (8.4% a year earlier), against 8.0% for England.
  • Against its intake, on the v2.0 intake terms, the area’s schools ran 0.16 points below what their pupils’ profiles predict over 2021/22 to 2024/25 (West Sussex -0.27 and Brighton and Hove +1.64).
  • Chichester High School runs more than a point above its prediction.
  • Chichester Free School runs more than a point below.

Suspensions were 19.8 episodes per 100 pupils in 2024/25, against 21.2 for England. One pupil can be suspended several times: 7.0 pupils per 100 were suspended at least once, about 2.8 times each, against 7.3 per 100 and 2.9 times in England. Reduced timetables take 0.45% of sessions, 1.6 times England’s rate.

School by school

Across England the two signals move together: schools whose absence runs below what their intake predicts tend to add more. Across Chichester’s schools the correlation between the two is -0.10 (-0.54 across England).

Questions for discussion

  1. Disadvantaged pupils. Their value added averages -1.6 points across Chichester’s schools against -1.6 for other pupils. What does Midhurst Rother College, above expectation for them, do that others could share?
  2. Attendance is a modest lever. Absence costs 0.5 points against England.
  3. Schools, not the place. No district effect survives each school’s own record on v2.0: the differences that matter are between schools.
  4. Practice. In Chichester, reduced timetables run at 1.6 times the national rate. These are choices made within schools.
  5. The authority. West Sussex’s own v2.0 effect for disadvantaged pupils is -2.6% (124th of 151), distinguishable from zero.

Notes on method

Value Added v2.0 is described on the method page. The absence-against-intake measure, the district test, the trust analysis, the attendance-counted-in value added and the scenarios are refitted on the v2.0 specification by R/04_place_and_absence.R. The absence series, reasons and suspensions are the original project’s (R/17_area_analysis.R). School districts come from each school’s postcode. The decomposition splits each area’s mean log Attainment 8 against England’s into one piece per model term, exactly, and converts to points at England’s mean. This page is generated from templates/area.qmd by R/03_area_pages.R; the format is recorded in AREA_REPORTS.md.
