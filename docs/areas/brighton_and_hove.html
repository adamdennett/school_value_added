Brighton and Hove

Value Added v2.0 for Brighton and Hove’s secondary schools.

NoteAbout this page

Value Added v2.0 for Brighton and Hove’s state-funded secondary schools, 2021/22 to 2024/25, measured against England (see the method), with the analyses of the original Sussex document refitted on the v2.0 model. Built from published DfE and Ofsted data; nothing uses pupil-level records.

Summary

  • 10 schools, with value added for all pupils averaging +1.6 points against England: 5 significantly above expectation and none below.
  • Disadvantaged pupils +0.5, non-disadvantaged +1.6; girls +1.1, boys +1.8.
  • Strongest: Varndean School (+3.4). Weakest: Patcham High School (-1.7).
  • Absence runs 1.6 points above what the intake predicts on average over 2021/22 to 2024/25 (4th highest of 152 authorities); Longhill High School, Brighton Aldridge Community Academy, Blatchington Mill School, Hove Park School and Sixth Form Centre, Portslade Aldridge Community Academy, Dorothy Stringer School and Cardinal Newman Catholic School more than a point above.
  • The authority’s own effect is +3.6% (5th of 152), distinguishable from zero for all pupils, and +4.1% (16th) for disadvantaged pupils.
  • Suspensions: 13.0 episodes per 100 pupils (England 21.2); 5.9 pupils per 100 suspended at least once (England 7.3). Reduced timetables run at 1.0 times the national rate.
  • Trusts that stand out nationally on v2.0: Aldridge Education (absence +11.7%).
  • Latest Ofsted: 5 report cards, 2 inspections with no overall grade, 2 graded inspections and 1 ungraded visit only.

How Brighton and Hove compares

What explains attainment in Brighton and Hove

For all pupils, Brighton and Hove is 0.9 points above England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • absence (-2.09);
  • local authority (+1.64);
  • neighbourhood education (+1.19);
  • prior attainment (KS2) (+0.51);
  • region (-0.47);
  • special educational needs (-0.45);
  • and the schools themselves (+0.64).

For disadvantaged pupils, Brighton and Hove is 1.7 points below England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • absence (-2.35);
  • local authority (+1.47);
  • region (-1.22);
  • prior attainment (KS2) (+0.44);
  • special educational needs (-0.33);
  • neighbourhood education (+0.29);
  • admissions (selection) (-0.27);
  • and the schools themselves (+0.39).

Place: authorities and districts

The district test adds a district level to the v2.0 model, with and without each school’s own record. An effect that disappears once schools are counted belongs to particular schools; one that survives is something the district’s schools share.

Brighton and Hove is a unitary authority, its own single district, so a district term would only duplicate the authority effect above; the test does not apply.

Trusts

The v2.0 model with a multi-academy trust level, allowing for each school’s own record and for how it came to be an academy, so trusts that take on struggling schools are not penalised for it. Nationally a trust moves results by about 2.4%, and absence by about 6.5%.

The disadvantaged gap, and girls and boys

No school is above expectation for its non-disadvantaged pupils while more than a point below it for its disadvantaged ones. Varndean School, Dorothy Stringer School, Portslade Aldridge Community Academy, Hove Park School and Sixth Form Centre, King’s School, Brighton Aldridge Community Academy and Blatchington Mill School are above expectation for both groups. Girls’ and boys’ value added differ by more than two points at Varndean School (girls +2.0, boys +4.3), Portslade Aldridge Community Academy (girls +1.9, boys +4.5) and Patcham High School (girls -3.3, boys -0.2).

Absence

  • 9.8% absence in autumn and spring 2025/26 (10.3% a year earlier), against 8.0% for England.
  • Against its intake, on the v2.0 intake terms, the area’s schools ran 1.64 points above what their pupils’ profiles predict over 2021/22 to 2024/25 (East Sussex +0.31 and West Sussex -0.27).
  • Longhill High School, Brighton Aldridge Community Academy, Blatchington Mill School, Hove Park School and Sixth Form Centre, Portslade Aldridge Community Academy, Dorothy Stringer School and Cardinal Newman Catholic School each run more than a point above their predictions.
  • King’s School runs more than a point below.

Suspensions were 13.0 episodes per 100 pupils in 2024/25, against 21.2 for England. One pupil can be suspended several times: 5.9 pupils per 100 were suspended at least once, about 2.2 times each, against 7.3 per 100 and 2.9 times in England. Reduced timetables take 0.29% of sessions, 1.0 times England’s rate.

School by school

Across England the two signals move together: schools whose absence runs below what their intake predicts tend to add more. Across Brighton and Hove’s schools the correlation between the two is -0.55 (-0.54 across England).

Questions for discussion

  1. Disadvantaged pupils. Their value added averages +0.5 points across Brighton and Hove’s schools against +1.6 for other pupils. What do Brighton Aldridge Community Academy, Hove Park School and Sixth Form Centre and Varndean School, above expectation for them, do that others could share?
  2. Attendance is a lever here. Absence costs 2.1 points against England, and the area’s schools run 1.6 points above what their intakes predict.
  3. Practice. Reduced timetables and suspensions are close to or below the national rate.
  4. The authority. Brighton and Hove’s own v2.0 effect for disadvantaged pupils is +4.1% (16th of 151), distinguishable from zero.

Notes on method

Value Added v2.0 is described on the method page. The absence-against-intake measure, the district test, the trust analysis, the attendance-counted-in value added and the scenarios are refitted on the v2.0 specification by R/04_place_and_absence.R. The absence series, reasons and suspensions are the original project’s (R/17_area_analysis.R). School districts come from each school’s postcode. The decomposition splits each area’s mean log Attainment 8 against England’s into one piece per model term, exactly, and converts to points at England’s mean. This page is generated from templates/area.qmd by R/03_area_pages.R; the format is recorded in AREA_REPORTS.md.
