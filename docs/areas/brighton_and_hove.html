Brighton and Hove

Value Added v2.0 for Brighton and Hove’s secondary schools.

NoteAbout this page

Value Added v2.0 for Brighton and Hove’s state-funded secondary schools, 2021/22 to 2024/25, measured against England: see the method. The original analysis, with absence, suspensions and trusts in depth, is here. Built from published DfE and Ofsted data; nothing uses pupil-level records.

Summary

  • 10 schools with value added for all pupils averaging +1.6 points against England: 5 significantly above significantly above expectation and none below.
  • Disadvantaged pupils: +0.5 points; non-disadvantaged pupils: +1.6.
  • Girls: +1.1 points; boys: +1.8.
  • Strongest: Varndean School (+3.4). Weakest: Patcham High School (-1.7).
  • The authority’s own effect is +3.6% (5th of 152), distinguishable from zero for all pupils, and +4.1% (16th) for disadvantaged pupils. Value added against England includes it.
  • Against the published v1 measure, the area’s schools move by +1.1 points on average (v2 against England minus v1 within the authority).
  • Latest Ofsted: 5 report cards, 2 inspection with no overall grades, 2 graded inspections and 1 ungraded visit only.

What explains the area’s results

For all pupils, Brighton and Hove’s two largest pieces are absence (-2.1) and local authority (+1.6), and its schools themselves account for +0.6 points. For disadvantaged pupils the two largest are absence (-2.3) and local authority (+1.5), and the schools +0.4.

Notes on method

Value added v2.0 is described on the method page. School districts come from each school’s postcode. The decomposition splits each area’s mean log Attainment 8 against England’s into one piece per model term, exactly, and converts to points at England’s mean.
