Crawley

Value Added v2.0 for the secondary schools of Crawley, West Sussex.

NoteAbout this page

Value Added v2.0 for Crawley’s state-funded secondary schools, 2021/22 to 2024/25, measured against England (see the method), with the analyses of the original Sussex document refitted on the v2.0 model. Built from published DfE and Ofsted data; nothing uses pupil-level records.

Summary

  • 7 schools, with value added for all pupils averaging -2.1 points against England: none significantly above expectation and 2 below.
  • Disadvantaged pupils -3.3, non-disadvantaged -2.5; girls -2.3, boys -1.9.
  • Strongest: Hazelwick School (-0.0). Weakest: Thomas Bennett Community College (-4.6).
  • Absence runs 0.3 points below what the intake predicts on average over 2021/22 to 2024/25; Holy Trinity CofE Secondary School, Crawley more than a point above.
  • District effects on the v2.0 model (Crawley for all pupils (-3.6%) and Crawley for disadvantaged pupils (-4.9%)) disappear once each school’s own record is counted: they belong to schools, not places.
  • Suspensions: 18.5 episodes per 100 pupils (England 21.2); 7.0 pupils per 100 suspended at least once (England 7.3). Reduced timetables run at 2.1 times the national rate.
  • Trusts that stand out nationally on v2.0: The Kemnal Academies Trust (all pupils -3.1% and absence +11.3%).
  • Latest Ofsted: 1 inspection with no overall grade, 4 graded inspections and 2 ungraded visits only.

How Crawley compares

What explains attainment in Crawley

For all pupils, Crawley is 4.7 points below England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • prior attainment (KS2) (-1.28);
  • neighbourhood education (-0.63);
  • region (-0.47);
  • pupil mobility (-0.46);
  • local authority (+0.37);
  • English as an additional language (+0.29);
  • and the schools themselves (-2.55).

For disadvantaged pupils, Crawley is 5.6 points below England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • region (-1.22);
  • local authority (-1.04);
  • prior attainment (KS2) (-0.83);
  • English as an additional language (+0.81);
  • pupil mobility (-0.39);
  • admissions (selection) (-0.27);
  • and the schools themselves (-2.31).

Place: authorities and districts

The district test adds a district level to the v2.0 model, with and without each school’s own record. An effect that disappears once schools are counted belongs to particular schools; one that survives is something the district’s schools share.

Without each school’s own record: Crawley for all pupils (-3.6%) and Crawley for disadvantaged pupils (-4.9%). With it, none remains: the differences belong to particular schools, not to the districts as places.

Trusts

The v2.0 model with a multi-academy trust level, allowing for each school’s own record and for how it came to be an academy, so trusts that take on struggling schools are not penalised for it. Nationally a trust moves results by about 2.4%, and absence by about 6.5%.

The disadvantaged gap, and girls and boys

No school is above expectation for its non-disadvantaged pupils while more than a point below it for its disadvantaged ones.

In no school do girls’ and boys’ value added differ by more than two points.

Absence

  • 8.3% absence in autumn and spring 2025/26 (8.8% a year earlier), against 8.0% for England.
  • Against its intake, on the v2.0 intake terms, the area’s schools ran 0.33 points below what their pupils’ profiles predict over 2021/22 to 2024/25 (West Sussex -0.27 and Brighton and Hove +1.64).
  • Holy Trinity CofE Secondary School, Crawley runs more than a point above its prediction.
  • Hazelwick School and St Wilfrid’s Catholic Comprehensive School, Crawley run more than a point below.

Suspensions were 18.5 episodes per 100 pupils in 2024/25, against 21.2 for England. One pupil can be suspended several times: 7.0 pupils per 100 were suspended at least once, about 2.7 times each, against 7.3 per 100 and 2.9 times in England. Reduced timetables take 0.60% of sessions, 2.1 times England’s rate.

School by school

Across England the two signals move together: schools whose absence runs below what their intake predicts tend to add more. Across Crawley’s schools the correlation between the two is -0.50 (-0.54 across England).

Questions for discussion

  1. Disadvantaged pupils. Their value added averages -3.3 points across Crawley’s schools against -2.5 for other pupils. No school here is above expectation for them; what do schools elsewhere with similar intakes do differently?
  2. Attendance is not the area’s main lever, though individual schools above their predicted absence are worth a look.
  3. Schools, not the place. No district effect survives each school’s own record on v2.0: the differences that matter are between schools.
  4. Practice. In Crawley, reduced timetables run at 2.1 times the national rate. These are choices made within schools.
  5. The authority. West Sussex’s own v2.0 effect for disadvantaged pupils is -2.6% (124th of 151), distinguishable from zero.

Notes on method

Value Added v2.0 is described on the method page. The absence-against-intake measure, the district test, the trust analysis, the attendance-counted-in value added and the scenarios are refitted on the v2.0 specification by R/04_place_and_absence.R. The absence series, reasons and suspensions are the original project’s (R/17_area_analysis.R). School districts come from each school’s postcode. The decomposition splits each area’s mean log Attainment 8 against England’s into one piece per model term, exactly, and converts to points at England’s mean. This page is generated from templates/area.qmd by R/03_area_pages.R; the format is recorded in AREA_REPORTS.md.
