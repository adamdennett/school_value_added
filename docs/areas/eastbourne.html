Eastbourne

Value Added v2.0 for the secondary schools of Eastbourne, East Sussex.

NoteAbout this page

Value Added v2.0 for Eastbourne’s state-funded secondary schools, 2021/22 to 2024/25, measured against England (see the method), with the analyses of the original Sussex document refitted on the v2.0 model. Built from published DfE and Ofsted data; nothing uses pupil-level records.

Summary

  • 6 schools, with value added for all pupils averaging -0.8 points against England: none significantly above expectation and 1 below.
  • Disadvantaged pupils -1.4, non-disadvantaged -0.6; girls -0.7, boys -0.8.
  • Strongest: St Catherine’s College (+1.4). Weakest: Gildredge House (-2.5).
  • Absence runs 0.4 points below what the intake predicts on average over 2021/22 to 2024/25; The Turing School more than a point above.
  • 0 of 6 schools add value with attendance at or better than their intake predicts; 1 adds value despite worse attendance, and 2 are below expectation with worse attendance (value added and attendance).
  • No district effect can be found on the v2.0 model.
  • Suspensions: 24.9 episodes per 100 pupils (England 21.2); 8.0 pupils per 100 suspended at least once (England 7.3). Reduced timetables run at 1.5 times the national rate.
  • Latest Ofsted: 2 report cards, 3 inspections with no overall grade and 1 graded inspection.
  • 2025/26, provisional: value added for all pupils averages +0.1 points across the area’s schools on DfE’s provisional results. This will be updated with the revised release in February 2027.

Who runs them

Value added and attendance

Two questions, kept apart. Does the school add value? Value Added v2.0 answers that with absence allowed for: a school with more absence is expected to score lower, so it is judged on what it achieves given its attendance. Does it hold attendance? Absence against intake answers that: how far its absence runs above or below what pupils like its own would predict. The chart sets one against the other. Neither contains the other, so the four corners mean what they say.

Of 6 schools, 0 add value with attendance at or better than their intake predicts, and 1 adds value despite worse attendance. 2 are below expectation with worse attendance, and 3 are below expectation despite attendance at or better than predicted. Across England the two are close to independent (correlation -0.07; 0.31 across Eastbourne’s schools). Adding value and holding attendance are different things, and plenty of schools do one without the other.

Value added with no allowance for absence. The map also offers value added from the model without its absence term. It does not excuse a school its absence: for every percentage point its absence runs above what its intake predicts, it loses about 1.1 Attainment 8 points against Value Added v2.0. It is the two axes above folded into one number, so it belongs on the map, not on this chart, where it would contain the absence it is plotted against.

What Eastbourne’s schools add, and what absence takes away

Value added already counts absence as part of a school’s context, so what absence moves and what the school moves are separate quantities rather than one counted twice. Below, the blue bar is what this area’s absence is worth against the England average of 8.6% in 2024/25, run through the national gradient; the purple bar is the area’s mean value added, which is measured against England already. The diamond is the two together. Absence here is full-year absence in 2024/25, the mean of schools; value added covers 2021/22 to 2024/25. How the bars are computed from the model is set out in the methodology.

Eastbourne’s schools ran at 9.4% absence in 2024/25 against England’s 8.6%, which on the national gradient is worth -0.9 Attainment 8 points. Their mean value added is -0.8, so the two together leave the area -1.8 points against England. Both are pulling the same way, downwards.

For disadvantaged pupils the same absence is worth -1.1 points of their Attainment 8 (mean 38.4) rather than -0.9 for pupils as a whole (47.5): the gradient is 41% steeper for them, so wherever attendance is the problem it is a sharper problem for the pupils with least room to absorb it.

The gradient is associational. It says what schools with this much absence look like once intake is accounted for, not what this area would gain by changing it.

How Eastbourne compares

What explains attainment in Eastbourne

For all pupils, Eastbourne is 4.2 points below England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • prior attainment (KS2) (-2.05);
  • neighbourhood education (-0.43);
  • region (-0.40);
  • local authority (+0.40);
  • disadvantage (-0.37);
  • absence (-0.36);
  • and the schools themselves (-0.98).

For disadvantaged pupils, Eastbourne is 4.1 points below England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • prior attainment (KS2) (-1.39);
  • region (-1.22);
  • absence (-0.36);
  • admissions (selection) (-0.27);
  • and the schools themselves (-0.95).

Place: authorities and districts

The district test adds a district level to the v2.0 model, with and without each school’s own record. An effect that disappears once schools are counted belongs to particular schools; one that survives is something the district’s schools share.

None of Eastbourne has a district effect distinguishable from zero on the v2.0 model.

Trusts

The v2.0 model with a multi-academy trust level, allowing for each school’s own record and for how it came to be an academy, so trusts that take on struggling schools are not penalised for it. Nationally a trust moves results by about 3.0%, and absence by about 6.5%.

The disadvantaged gap, and girls and boys

Of 6 schools:

  • 1 is above expectation for both disadvantaged and non-disadvantaged pupils.
  • 1 is above expectation for non-disadvantaged pupils but more than a point below it for disadvantaged ones: the gap schools.
  • 1 has girls’ and boys’ value added more than two points apart.

Absence

  • 8.8% absence in autumn and spring 2025/26 (8.7% a year earlier), against 8.0% for England. These are all sessions together, so a large school counts for more than a small one; the full-year 2024/25 figures in what absence takes away are a mean of schools.
  • Against its intake, on the v2.0 intake terms, the area’s schools ran 0.37 points below what their pupils’ profiles predict over 2021/22 to 2024/25 (East Sussex +0.31 and Brighton and Hove +1.64).
  • The Turing School runs more than a point above its prediction.
  • The Cavendish School, Gildredge House and Ratton School run more than a point below.

Suspensions were 24.9 episodes per 100 pupils in 2024/25, against 21.2 for England. One pupil can be suspended several times: 8.0 pupils per 100 were suspended at least once, about 3.1 times each, against 7.3 per 100 and 2.9 times in England. Reduced timetables take 0.45% of sessions, 1.5 times England’s rate.

Over time

Value added is computed for every year separately, then averaged. A single year moves a good deal; a three-year moving average centred on a year (that year, the one before and the one after) shows the trajectory more steadily. Years at the ends use the years they have. 2025/26 is provisional and shown as a hollow point. The explorer lets you choose the centre year and the window.

On the three-year centred average from 2022/23 to 2024/25, the largest rises are at The Eastbourne Academy (+2.4) and Gildredge House (+2.1) and the largest falls at St Catherine’s College (-0.8) and The Cavendish School (-1.0). The later windows include the provisional 2025/26 year.

Questions for discussion

  1. Disadvantaged pupils. Their value added averages -1.4 points across Eastbourne’s schools against -0.6 for other pupils. What do St Catherine’s College and The Cavendish School, above expectation for them, do that others could share?
  2. Attendance is a modest lever. Absence costs 0.4 points against England.
  3. Schools, not the place. No district effect survives each school’s own record on v2.0: the differences that matter are between schools.
  4. Practice. In Eastbourne, reduced timetables run at 1.5 times the national rate. These are choices made within schools.
  5. The authority. East Sussex’s own v2.0 effect for disadvantaged pupils is +0.6% (65th of 151), not distinguishable from zero.

Notes on method

Value Added v2.0 is described on the method page. The absence-against-intake measure, the district test, the trust analysis, the attendance-counted-in value added and the scenarios are refitted on the v2.0 specification by R/04_place_and_absence.R. The absence series, reasons and suspensions are the original project’s (R/17_area_analysis.R). School districts come from each school’s postcode. The decomposition splits each area’s mean log Attainment 8 against England’s into one piece per model term, exactly, and converts to points at England’s mean. This page is generated from templates/area.qmd by R/03_area_pages.R; the format is recorded in AREA_REPORTS.md.
