Wealden

Value Added v2.0 for the secondary schools of Wealden, East Sussex.

NoteAbout this page

Value Added v2.0 for Wealden’s state-funded secondary schools, 2021/22 to 2024/25, measured against England (see the method), with the analyses of the original Sussex document refitted on the v2.0 model. Built from published DfE and Ofsted data; nothing uses pupil-level records.

Summary

  • 6 schools, with value added for all pupils averaging +0.6 points against England: 2 significantly above expectation and 1 below.
  • Disadvantaged pupils +0.8, non-disadvantaged +0.1; girls +0.5, boys +0.5.
  • Strongest: Beacon Academy (+4.3). Weakest: Hailsham Community College (-3.3).
  • Absence runs 0.4 points above what the intake predicts on average over 2021/22 to 2024/25; Uckfield College more than a point above.
  • No district effect can be found on the v2.0 model.
  • Suspensions: 21.9 episodes per 100 pupils (England 21.2); 7.3 pupils per 100 suspended at least once (England 7.3). Reduced timetables run at 1.9 times the national rate.
  • Latest Ofsted: 4 graded inspections and 2 ungraded visits only.
  • 2025/26, provisional: value added for all pupils averages +0.9 points across the area’s schools on DfE’s provisional results. This will be updated with the revised release in February 2027.

How Wealden compares

What explains attainment in Wealden

For all pupils, Wealden is 0.5 points above England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • absence (-0.84);
  • disadvantage (+0.66);
  • region (-0.47);
  • English as an additional language (-0.45);
  • local authority (+0.43);
  • special educational needs (+0.38);
  • and the schools themselves (+0.57).

For disadvantaged pupils, Wealden is 1.6 points below England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • English as an additional language (-1.22);
  • region (-1.22);
  • absence (-0.92);
  • disadvantage (-0.39);
  • admissions (selection) (-0.27);
  • and the schools themselves (+1.99).

Place: authorities and districts

The district test adds a district level to the v2.0 model, with and without each school’s own record. An effect that disappears once schools are counted belongs to particular schools; one that survives is something the district’s schools share.

None of Wealden has a district effect distinguishable from zero on the v2.0 model.

Trusts

The v2.0 model with a multi-academy trust level, allowing for each school’s own record and for how it came to be an academy, so trusts that take on struggling schools are not penalised for it. Nationally a trust moves results by about 2.4%, and absence by about 6.5%.

The disadvantaged gap, and girls and boys

No school is above expectation for its non-disadvantaged pupils while more than a point below it for its disadvantaged ones. Beacon Academy and Heathfield Community College are above expectation for both groups. In no school do girls’ and boys’ value added differ by more than two points.

Absence

  • 9.4% absence in autumn and spring 2025/26 (9.3% a year earlier), against 8.0% for England.
  • Against its intake, on the v2.0 intake terms, the area’s schools ran 0.41 points above what their pupils’ profiles predict over 2021/22 to 2024/25 (East Sussex +0.31 and Brighton and Hove +1.64).
  • Uckfield College runs more than a point above its prediction.
  • Willingdon Community School runs more than a point below.

Suspensions were 21.9 episodes per 100 pupils in 2024/25, against 21.2 for England. One pupil can be suspended several times: 7.3 pupils per 100 were suspended at least once, about 3.0 times each, against 7.3 per 100 and 2.9 times in England. Reduced timetables take 0.56% of sessions, 1.9 times England’s rate.

School by school

Across England the two signals move together: schools whose absence runs below what their intake predicts tend to add more. Across Wealden’s schools the correlation between the two is -0.04 (-0.54 across England).

Questions for discussion

  1. Disadvantaged pupils. Their value added averages +0.8 points across Wealden’s schools against +0.1 for other pupils. What do Beacon Academy, Heathfield Community College and Uplands Academy, above expectation for them, do that others could share?
  2. Attendance is a modest lever. Absence costs 0.8 points against England.
  3. Schools, not the place. No district effect survives each school’s own record on v2.0: the differences that matter are between schools.
  4. Practice. In Wealden, reduced timetables run at 1.9 times the national rate. These are choices made within schools.
  5. The authority. East Sussex’s own v2.0 effect for disadvantaged pupils is +0.5% (66th of 151), not distinguishable from zero.

Notes on method

Value Added v2.0 is described on the method page. The absence-against-intake measure, the district test, the trust analysis, the attendance-counted-in value added and the scenarios are refitted on the v2.0 specification by R/04_place_and_absence.R. The absence series, reasons and suspensions are the original project’s (R/17_area_analysis.R). School districts come from each school’s postcode. The decomposition splits each area’s mean log Attainment 8 against England’s into one piece per model term, exactly, and converts to points at England’s mean. This page is generated from templates/area.qmd by R/03_area_pages.R; the format is recorded in AREA_REPORTS.md.
