Sussex

Value Added v2.0 across East Sussex, West Sussex and Brighton and Hove.

NoteAbout this page

Value Added v2.0 for Sussex’s state-funded secondary schools, 2021/22 to 2024/25, measured against England (see the method), with the analyses of the original Sussex document refitted on the v2.0 model. Built from published DfE and Ofsted data; nothing uses pupil-level records.

Summary

  • 76 schools, with value added for all pupils averaging +0.2 points against England: 18 significantly above expectation and 8 below.
  • Disadvantaged pupils -1.0, non-disadvantaged +0.1; girls +0.0, boys +0.4.
  • Strongest: Priory School (+5.0). Weakest: The St Leonards Academy (-5.5).
  • Absence runs 0.2 points above what the intake predicts on average over 2021/22 to 2024/25; Longhill High School, Brighton Aldridge Community Academy, The St Leonards Academy, The Hastings Academy, Seahaven Academy, The Turing School, Rye College, The Littlehampton Academy, Blatchington Mill School, Hove Park School and Sixth Form Centre, Portslade Aldridge Community Academy, Dorothy Stringer School, Uckfield College, Holy Trinity CofE Secondary School, Crawley, Chichester High School, Cardinal Newman Catholic School, Priory School and The Burgess Hill Academy more than a point above.
  • Suspensions: 19.4 episodes per 100 pupils (England 21.2); 6.8 pupils per 100 suspended at least once (England 7.3). Reduced timetables run at 1.6 times the national rate.
  • Trusts that stand out nationally on v2.0: Aldridge Education (absence +11.7%), Ark Schools (disadvantaged pupils +4.4% and absence -9.0%), The Kemnal Academies Trust (all pupils -3.1% and absence +11.3%) and United Learning Trust (all pupils +6.3% and disadvantaged pupils +8.6%).
  • Latest Ofsted: 18 report cards, 12 inspections with no overall grade, 28 graded inspections and 18 ungraded visits only.
  • 2025/26, provisional: value added for all pupils averages +0.8 points across the area’s schools on DfE’s provisional results. This will be updated with the revised release in February 2027.

How Sussex compares

What explains attainment in Sussex

For all pupils, Sussex is 0.7 points below England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • absence (-0.65);
  • local authority (+0.56);
  • region (-0.47);
  • prior attainment (KS2) (-0.46);
  • disadvantage (+0.42);
  • and the schools themselves (+0.13).

For disadvantaged pupils, Sussex is 3.5 points below England. The pieces of at least a quarter of a point:

  • region (-1.22);
  • absence (-0.70);
  • English as an additional language (-0.36);
  • local authority (-0.29);
  • admissions (selection) (-0.27);
  • disadvantage (-0.26);
  • prior attainment (KS2) (-0.25);
  • and the schools themselves (+0.02).

Place: authorities and districts

The district test adds a district level to the v2.0 model, with and without each school’s own record. An effect that disappears once schools are counted belongs to particular schools; one that survives is something the district’s schools share.

Without each school’s own record: Crawley for all pupils (-3.6%), Hastings for all pupils (-4.4%), Lewes for all pupils (+4.9%), Crawley for disadvantaged pupils (-4.9%), Hastings for disadvantaged pupils (-5.4%) and Hastings for absence (+8.5%). With it, none remains: the differences belong to particular schools, not to the districts as places.

Trusts

The v2.0 model with a multi-academy trust level, allowing for each school’s own record and for how it came to be an academy, so trusts that take on struggling schools are not penalised for it. Nationally a trust moves results by about 2.4%, and absence by about 6.5%.

School by school

Across England the two signals move together: schools whose absence runs below what their intake predicts tend to add more. Across Sussex’s schools the correlation between the two is -0.56 (-0.54 across England).
