School Information System

Version 2.1, on Value Added v2.1: a school finder, twins, observed against predicted, the policy simulator, area overviews, travel and distance, pupil numbers and school finances, in one app.

The School Information System replaces this page’s earlier in-browser simulator. It sets Attainment 8 beside Value Added v2.1 for all, disadvantaged and non-disadvantaged pupils (with girls and boys), over four years, a single year or a moving average, for every state secondary school in England. Its tabs:

  • School Finder: a school by area, name or map, with its results, value added and absence against expected, year by year.
  • Find My Twin: the schools most like it in intake and setting, and the look-alikes that do better.
  • Observed vs Predicted: every school’s Attainment 8 and absence against what its intake predicts.
  • Policy Simulator: what the model expects if a school’s, or a whole area’s, absence or intake were different (how it works is below).
  • Area Overview: a region, combined or mayoral authority, local authority, district or academy trust.
  • Travel and Distance: distance and journey times to school, and walking catchments for every state secondary from the national routing.
  • Pupils and Places (new in 2.1): pupils by year group since 2015/16, the Year 7 intakes to 2032/33, and schools opened and closed, for an authority, a region or England (the method).
  • School Finances (new in 2.1): deficits, reserves and income per pupil against England, by authority, region and Sussex district, and counts of open schools showing the warning signs that came before closures, with no school named (the method).

Open the School Information System in a new window

The app runs on Posit Connect Cloud and may take a few seconds to wake up. Its code is in app/ and its data are built by R/40_app_data.R from the files behind this site.

How the simulator works

The simulator uses the fitted Value Added v2.1 models, one for each group of pupils. Each model explains the log of a school’s Attainment 8 by its intake and setting, so every lever changes a school’s expected result by a percentage:

\[\text{Attainment 8}_\text{new} = \text{Attainment 8}_\text{latest} \times \exp\Big(\sum_j \beta_j \, \Delta x_j\Big)\]

where \(\Delta x_j\) is the change in each lever (in logs for absence, disadvantage and EAL, which enter the model as logs) and \(\beta_j\) is its coefficient for that group. Everything else about the school stays as it was, including what the model cannot explain: its value added. A school starts from its latest published result (2024/25 for most), not from its prediction.

For all pupils, cutting a school’s absence by a tenth (say from 9% to 8.1%) goes with Attainment 8 2.5% higher, about 1.2 points at the average school. For disadvantaged pupils it is 3.5%, and for non-disadvantaged pupils 1.9%. The same cut in absence helps disadvantaged pupils more, so it narrows the gap.

In area mode, every school in the area gets the same change, and the area’s result is the average of its schools’ results weighted by their number of pupils.

What it can and cannot tell you

  • It is the model’s arithmetic, not a forecast. The coefficients describe how results differ between schools with different absence and intake, all else equal. They are not experiments. A school that cut its absence would not necessarily gain exactly this, and how it cut it would matter.
  • Absence is not a dial. The absence lever says what better attendance would be worth, not how to get it. See How school absence changes for what moves it.
  • The intake levers are not policies. They show how much of a school’s result is its pupils. Moving them is a way of seeing that, or of thinking through an admissions change, not something a school can do.
  • The groups are modelled separately. Each group has its own coefficients, so the same change can do more for one group than another. The all-pupils result is its own model, not an average of the others.

What changed from the original

The original School Attainment Policy Simulator (v1.0) ran on the first model. This version follows the review’s recommendations:

  • No Ofsted grade or workforce levers. They are no longer in the expectation.
  • New levers: SEN support, EHC plans, pupil mobility and neighbourhood education.
  • Value added beside Attainment 8, for girls and boys as well as disadvantaged and other pupils, over four years or year by year.
  • Area mode: the same change across every school in a district, authority, combined authority, region, trust or England.
  • Absence against expected and travel and distance are new.
