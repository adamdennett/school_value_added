I’ve assembled a selective research map of UK school absence over roughly the last decade, with England as the main focus and Scotland and Wales included where useful: [Open the research map](Elicit%20-%20UK%20School%20Absence%20Research%20Map%2C%202016%E2%80%932026.md).

The central picture is that absence reflects interacting health, mental-health, family, peer and school factors—not one cause—and is patterned by age, poverty and SEND. Absence is associated with lower attainment, with some school years more vulnerable and absence accounting for part, but not all, of socioeconomic attainment gaps. [^1][^2][^3][^4] The post-COVID explanation remains unsettled: UK qualitative studies describe anxiety, disrupted routines, SEND provision and home-school relationships, while comparative PISA evidence does not find that longer school closures explain the rise in teenage skipping. [^5][^6] The latest accredited England statistics located here show overall and persistent absence improving in 2024/25 but remaining above pre-pandemic levels, while severe absence continued to increase. [^2] The key gap is nationally representative longitudinal research that can measure the relative contribution of the different causes, especially for severe absence and pupils with SEND.
